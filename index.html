BEFORE YOU BUY

What to check besides the discount

A bigger percentage is not automatically a better deal. Research buyers usually compare these points before they use a code.

  • Certificate of analysis (COA)

    A lot-specific report that should match the product and batch you receive. Look for a clear date, lot number and the lab that produced it.

  • HPLC purity and mass spec

    HPLC reports a purity percentage and mass spectrometry confirms identity. A purity figure with no lot or lab behind it tells you little.

  • Third-party testing

    Independent testing is stronger evidence than a vendor’s own claim, especially when the report can be traced to a named laboratory.

  • Price per mg

    Divide the price after the coupon, plus shipping, by the milligrams in the vial. A cheaper-looking vial can cost more per mg.

  • Form and storage

    Most research peptides ship as a lyophilized (freeze-dried) powder. Check storage guidance and how the vendor packs and ships it.

  • Policies

    Read shipping times, returns and payment options before you order. They differ more between vendors than the discounts do.

Are peptide discount codes legit?

The codes on this site are listed as the vendors publish them, and each shows its status. A code can still stop working when a vendor changes or ends it, so confirm the total in your cart before you pay. Be wary of coupon pages that show hundreds of codes with no dates or vendor details.

How do I know a code still works?

Active means the code was listed as working in our records. Vendors can change or end a code at any time, so apply it in your cart and confirm the total changes before you pay.

Why is my peptide coupon code not working?

The usual causes are an expired code, a minimum order size, a code limited to certain products, a code that cannot be combined with a sale or membership price, and a typo or extra space. Check the offer type in the table first, then try the code on a regular-priced item.

What is a sitewide code compared with an eligible-products code?

A sitewide code takes its percentage off the whole cart. An eligible-products code only applies to items the vendor includes, so sale items, bundles or some compounds may be excluded. The Offer column shows which type each code is.

Is the biggest discount always the cheapest option?

No. A larger percentage on a higher list price, or a vendor with costly shipping, can cost more per milligram than a smaller discount elsewhere. Use the price per mg calculator to compare two vendors after the coupon and shipping.

How do I compare peptide prices fairly?

Convert every offer to price per mg: take the price after the coupon, add shipping, and divide by the milligrams in the vial. Then compare vendors on the same compound and the same vial size.

What is a COA, and why does it matter?

A certificate of analysis (COA) is a lab report for a specific batch. It usually lists the HPLC purity and the identity result from mass spectrometry. Check that the lot number, date and testing lab are clear, and that the report matches the product you ordered.

Are research peptides legal, and are they for human use?

Research peptides are sold for laboratory research use only. They are not approved medicines and are not for human or veterinary use. Rules differ by country, and regulators have acted against some sellers, so check the law where you live. This site gives no medical or legal advice.
